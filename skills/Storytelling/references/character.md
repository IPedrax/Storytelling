# Character

A character is not a description. A character is **a person who wants something and is wrong
about how to get it.** Everything below serves that sentence.

## The spine

Five parts, in causal order. Build them in this order; each one produces the next.

| Part | Question | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ghost** | What happened to them before page one? | A specific event, not a mood. Can be a *good* event misread. |
| **Wound** | What did it break or teach? | The damage the Ghost left behind. |
| **Lie** | What false rule about the world did they take from it? | Stated in first person: *"If I need anyone, I get left."* |
| **Want** | What are they chasing on the page? | Concrete, visible, pursuable scene to scene. Comes from the Lie. |
| **Need** | What would actually heal them? | The Truth that contradicts the Lie. They resist it. |

The Lie was **adaptive once**. It genuinely protected them at the time. That's why it's hard to
drop and why the reader forgives it. A Lie that was never useful is just stupidity.

**Want and Need must be in tension.** If getting the Want also delivers the Need, there is no
arc, only errands. The climax is usually the moment they must trade one for the other.

## Arc shapes

- **Positive**: believes the Lie, is battered by evidence, accepts the Truth, changes. Costs them the Want.
- **Flat**: already holds the Truth; the *world* believes the Lie. They change everyone else. (Needs a corrupt world to push against, or they're inert.)
- **Negative**: three flavors: they refuse the Truth and fall (*disillusionment*), they trade a Lie for a worse one (*corruption*), or they see the Truth and choose against it (*fall*). The last is the strongest, because it requires knowing.

Pick deliberately. A character with no arc is fine *if* someone near them has one; a cast where
nobody changes is a situation, not a story.

## Contradiction is the whole trick

Complexity lives in **the gap between professed identity and verified action.** Not in traits.

- Give every character one thing they say about themselves that their behavior disproves.
- Then give them one thing they'd never claim that their behavior proves.
- The reader trusts action over speech, always. Use that gap on purpose.

Examples of live contradiction: the pacifist who is *good* at violence and hates that they enjoy
the competence; the devout one whose faith is real but whose prayers are bargaining; the
generous host who cannot accept a gift.

Traits without contradiction produce a résumé. "Brave, loyal, quick-witted" is a job posting.

## Voice

Voice is not accent or catchphrase. Voice is **what this person notices, what they refuse to
say, and how they fill silence.**

Four dials to set per character:

1. **Attention**: walking into a room, what do they clock first? Exits? Prices? Who's lying? Who's hurt? This alone separates two POV characters more than any dialect.
2. **Register**: sentence length, abstraction level, whether they hedge. A field surgeon and a courtier describe the same wound differently and neither is "wrong".
3. **Taboo**: the words and topics they will not touch. Everyone has a subject they go quiet or clever about. That silence is characterization.
4. **Default under pressure**: joke, attack, comply, freeze, negotiate, explain. Pick one; it's the reflex that shows before thought does.

Test: cover the dialogue tags. If you can't tell who's speaking, they don't have voices yet.

## Interiority and subtext

**Interiority** is thought plus body together. Not "she was scared", but what she *thought about*
while her hands did something. Thought alone floats; body alone is mime.

**Subtext** is the conversation under the conversation. Dialogue is the performance a character
gives the world; the truth happens inside. A character who wants something but cannot ask
directly generates subtext for free: every "innocent" line is doing errand work.

The reliable move: character insists on a state, body contradicts it. *"I'm fine," she said, and
set the glass down hard enough to crack the base.* Never explain the crack.

## Relationships

Cast a character's relationships off their **Wound**, not off the plot's staffing needs. Every
significant relationship should do one of three jobs:

- **Mirror**: has the same Lie, further along. Shows what happens if they don't change.
- **Challenge**: embodies the Truth. Cannot be dismissed, which is why they're infuriating.
- **Exploit**: profits from the Lie. Needs them to stay broken.

A cast built this way generates subplot without inventing errands.

## Groups of characters

A cast is not a list of people; it's **a set of pressures that don't cancel out.**

- No two characters should solve the same problem the same way. If they do, merge them; the story gets shorter and better.
- Every member should be able to be *right* in at least one argument the protagonist loses.
- Loyalty needs a price tag. Ask of each member: what would make them leave? If the answer is "nothing", they're furniture.

## Depth: `C-` and `N-`

An NPC is not a different kind of creature from a character. It's the same creature built to a
different depth, and the depth is a **budget**, not a quality bar. Spend it where the story looks.

- **`N-`, `tier: background`**: a name and a function. The ferryman, the third guard. One concrete detail is the entire budget: *the ferryman won't take coin in his left hand.* No spine, no arc. Resist giving them one.
- **`N-`, `tier: minor`**: appears more than once and wants something small that crosses the plot. Needs a **want** and **one contradiction**. Nothing else. A minor character carrying a full ghost/lie/want/need spine is a main-cast character you haven't admitted to yet, and they will unbalance the book.
- **`C-`**: the full spine, an arc, a voice with all four dials set.

At the lower depths, **one specific, useless detail** reads as a whole person where three generic
traits read as none. Not "gruff, scarred, loyal", but "he apologizes to the horses." The reader
fills in the rest and does it better than you would.

Promotion is normal and cheap: rename `N-Doorkeeper` to `C-Doorkeeper` and Obsidian repoints
every link in the vault. Do it when a background character keeps pulling scenes toward them;
that is the story telling you where its energy is. Never in advance.

The reverse move is legitimate too. A `C-` who never earns their arc should be demoted or merged
into someone else, not carried. A cast is a budget as well.

## Aliveness checks

Run these when a character feels flat. Name the one that fails.

- **Agency**: do they ever cause the plot, or only absorb it? Count the turns they *drive*. Zero means passenger.
- **Wrongness**: are they ever wrong in a way the story doesn't immediately punish or excuse?
- **Off-page life**: what were they doing the week before the story? What will they do the week after it ends?
- **Appetite**: what do they enjoy that has nothing to do with the plot? People with no pleasures read as functions.
- **Refusal**: is there something they will not do even to win? That line *is* the character.
- **Cost of the Want**: what have they already given up for it? Wanting nothing at stake is a preference, not a want.
- **Somebody's wrong about them**: does at least one other character misread them? Being seen fully by everyone flattens a person.

## Failure modes

- **Backstory instead of behavior.** Pages of history, no present-tense decisions. History matters only where it makes them do something odd *now*.
- **Trauma as personality.** The Ghost explains the Lie; it does not replace a person. They still have opinions about food.
- **Competence with no cost.** Skill should have eaten something: time, a relationship, a body part, a childhood.
- **The reasonable protagonist.** A character who always makes the sensible call cannot be surprising. Let the Lie make them stupid at exactly one predictable pressure point.
- **Cast of agreement.** Everyone likes each other and shares goals. Nothing generates.

## Note shape

Organic: one file until it bursts. `C-Name.md` starts as:

```markdown
---
language: pt-BR
world: W-Kaldros        # omit if rootless
---

# C-Name

One line: who they are and what they want.

## Spine
Ghost / Wound / Lie (in their words) / Want / Need / arc shape.

## Contradiction
The claim, and the behavior that disproves it.

## Voice
Attention · register · taboo · default under pressure.

## People
[[C-Other]]: mirror/challenge/exploit, and the specific history.

## Open
Questions not yet answered. Keep them here instead of inventing filler.
```

Split out `Voice.md`, `History.md`, `Relationships.md` only when a section genuinely outgrows
the page. Leave a link behind when you do.
